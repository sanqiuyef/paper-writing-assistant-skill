# General Academic Paper Section Templates

## 1. Abstract

Use the sequence context–problem–methods/materials–main findings–conclusion and scope. Report only supplied or verifiable evidence. If results are unavailable, retain `{result pending}` rather than inventing numbers. Do not introduce a contribution absent from the paper.

## 2. Introduction

Define the problem with verifiable evidence; synthesize what prior work has resolved; state a testable gap and why it matters; then specify the research question, scope, and evidence-linked contributions. Avoid a paper-by-paper catalogue.

Placeholders: `{research object}`, `{problem}`, `{gap}`, `{research question}`, `{contributions}`.

## 3. Related Work

Organize two to four research streams. For each stream, cover the dominant approach, representative evidence, disagreement or boundary, and relevance to this study. Cite only verified sources. End with a positioning matrix covering object, method, data, evaluation, and scope.

## 4. Methodology

Describe study design, data or materials, task/variable definitions, analysis steps, quality control, and reproducibility. Explain variables, units, and assumptions after equations. Separate prespecified procedures from post-hoc interpretation.

## 5. Experimental Design

Specify study objects, controls or baselines, scenarios or treatments, metrics, and procedure. Map each experiment to a research question and define the decision criterion in advance. For non-experimental work, rename this section Analysis Design or merge it into Methodology.

## 6. Results and Analysis

Report results by research question. Present evidence before interpretation and state sample size, units, uncertainty, and comparison baseline where applicable. Keep observed results separate from mechanism claims.

## 7. Discussion

Explain how the findings answer the research question, compare them with prior work, discuss plausible mechanisms and implications, and state limitations and scope. Link each limitation to the conclusion it may affect.

## 8. Conclusion

Answer the research question directly, summarize evidence-backed contributions, and state non-transferable boundaries. Derive future work from identified limitations. Introduce no new data or untested claims.
