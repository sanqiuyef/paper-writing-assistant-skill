# Engineering Case-Study Paper Section Templates

## 1. Abstract

State the practical problem, study area or engineering object, data and model, scenarios, main spatiotemporal findings, and management implication. Use verified results and avoid generalizing one case into a universal law.

## 2. Introduction

Start from real risk and decision need, define the scope of existing models or interventions, identify a local gap in data, scale, scenario, or integrated indicators, and state the case objectives and transferable value.

## 3. Related Work

Organize around mechanisms, common models or assessments, comparable cases, and decision applications. Compare local conditions, data resolution, coupling, validation evidence, and risk indicators rather than model names alone.

## 4. Methodology

Recommended subsections: study area; data source, date, and resolution; model construction; parameters, boundaries, and assumptions; calibration and validation; scenario design; risk or performance indicators. Keep validation evidence independent from model inputs.

## 5. Experimental Design

Define reference and comparison scenarios, rationale, controlled variables, parameter ranges, boundary conditions, and decision thresholds. Include model validation and sensitivity analysis for uncertain influential parameters. Do not calibrate and validate on the same evidence.

## 6. Results and Analysis

Report validation first, followed by temporal evolution, spatial patterns, scenario differences, drivers, and risk zoning. State units and calculation conventions for areas, proportions, depths, durations, or effect sizes. Make the inference from risk evidence to recommendation explicit.

## 7. Discussion

Interpret mechanisms and engineering/governance implications, compare with similar areas, and explain how resolution, boundaries, omitted processes, and scenario assumptions affect conclusions. Separate locally dependent findings from transferable procedures.

## 8. Conclusion

Summarize case findings and actionable recommendations while stating geographic, scale, and scenario boundaries. Do not equate decision relevance with demonstrated real-world impact.
