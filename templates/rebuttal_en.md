# Response to Reviewers Template (English)

> Usage: respond point by point; answer every comment, none skipped. Every "changed" claim must match an actual change in the manuscript.
> Source: adapted from scipilot-writing-skill v1.0.0 (MIT License) rebuttal playbook.

## Opening: thanks + summary of major changes

Dear Editor and Reviewers,

We thank the reviewers for their constructive comments, which have substantially improved the manuscript. We have addressed every point below. The major changes include: {{summarize 2–4 most important revisions}}. All revisions are marked in {{marking convention, e.g., red / track changes}} in the revised manuscript.

## Per-comment skeleton (duplicate for each comment)

**Comment {{number}} (Reviewer {{X}}):** {{quote the reviewer's comment verbatim}}

**Response:** We thank the reviewer for this point. {{response body}}.

- Accepted and revised: We {{what was specifically done}} (see Section {{X}}, p. {{Y}}, lines {{Z}}): "{{quote the revised key sentence}}".
- Respectfully disagreed: We appreciate this perspective. However, based on {{data / literature / logical grounds}}, we believe {{reason}}. To address the concern, we have {{compromise, e.g., added a limitation statement rather than redoing the analysis}}.
- Out of scope: This question concerns {{scope}}, which is beyond the focus of the present study. We have noted this in the Discussion/Future work {{section reference}} and plan to pursue {{direction}} in follow-up work.

## Strategy for the three comment types

| Comment type | Strategy |
|:---|:---|
| Actionable (new experiment/analysis/rewrite/references) | State clearly "done", give the new result or new text, and locate it precisely (page/line) |
| Disagreed | Politely rebut with evidence, not deference; compromise where reasonable (e.g., a limitation statement instead of a redo) |
| Structural / out of scope | Explain why it exceeds the paper's scope, or commit to future work; be candid about limitations |

## Key points and common mistakes

**Key points**
- Conceding a fair criticism increases credibility; it is not weakness
- Rebut the issue, not the person; support with data, literature, or logic
- When reviewers conflict, explain how you reconciled the trade-off
- Visually distinguish three kinds of content: reviewer comment / author response / revised manuscript text

**Common mistakes**
- Saying "revised as suggested" without giving a location
- Skipping any comment (even "we thank the reviewer; this has been corrected" counts as a response)
- Arguing with reviewers
- Agreeing in words without actually changing the manuscript
- Contradicting yourself between responses
